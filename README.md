<h1><strong>How Does a Man Fall in Love? Understanding the Three Key Stages</strong></h1>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Falling in love feels different from the inside than it looks from the outside, and that is especially true when you are trying to read a man's feelings.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The most widely cited answer to "what are the three stages of a man falling in love?" comes from anthropologist Helen Fisher's research on romantic love: lust, attraction and attachment.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This guide explains each of those stages of falling in love for a man, how fast men tend to move through them, what usually comes after the honeymoon phase, and where popular ideas like the "three loves theory" fit in.</span></p>
<p><em><span style="font-weight: 400;">This article is general information about relationship psychology, not personal or professional advice. People differ, and no stage list predicts what one particular man feels.</span></em></p>
<h2><strong>The short answer</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">What are the</span><a href="https://yauld-emu-12znf56.mystrikingly.com/"><span style="font-weight: 400;"> three stages of men falling in love</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">? Researchers describe three stages of falling in love: lust, attraction and attachment.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In stages of falling in love with psychology research, these come mainly from Fisher's work. For a man, they usually look like this:</span></p>
<ol>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Lust:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> physical interest and a drive to get closer.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Attraction:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> a focused, intense interest in one person, often with obsessive thoughts.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Attachment:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> calmer, deeper bonding and a wish to commit.</span></li>
</ol>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">One honest caveat. Fisher's model describes how people in general fall in love.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It does not claim that men and women go through different stages, so "stages of love for a man" is the same framework seen through one person's behavior.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Stage 1: Lust</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lust comes first in Fisher's model.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">According to a summary of her work, it is driven by sex hormones and a craving for sexual gratification.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For a man, this stage often looks like noticing someone physically, wanting to spend time near them, and feeling drawn to flirt.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lust alone is not love.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">You can feel lust for someone with no emotional bond at all, which is why researchers treat it as its own system.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Many relationships stall here, and many that start here go on to the next stage.</span></p>

<h2><strong>Stage 2: Attraction</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Attraction is the stage most people picture when they imagine falling in love.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Fisher's team scanned the brains of people who were madly in love and found activity in reward areas, and a summary from Experience Life notes that lovers are not just single-minded about the person, they are obsessed.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For a man, this might look like thinking about her constantly, rearranging his schedule, feeling nervous before seeing her, and seeing her as unique.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This is also the stage many people call the honeymoon phase. A science writer for the British Council explains the brain-chemistry side of all three stages in the</span><a href="https://www.britishcouncil.org/voices-magazine/three-things-that-happen-when-we-fall-in-love"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">British Council's guide to love's three stages</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, including why the blissful state cannot last forever.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Stage 3: Attachment</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The third stage is attachment.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A summary of Fisher's model describes it as far less volatile than the earlier stages, marked by calm and a focus on long-term bonding and commitment.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Attachment is linked to the bonding chemicals oxytocin and vasopressin, according to a neurobiology overview.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For a man, attachment tends to show up as steadiness: wanting to build a life together, being comfortable in quiet moments, and treating the relationship as a priority.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It is less thrilling than stage two, and it is what sustains most long relationships.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Three loves theory: first, second and third love</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Many people search for the three loves theory, also written as 3 loves theory, three love theory or 3 loves in life.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In popular form, it says most people have three major loves.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The first love is driven by lust and physical attraction.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The second love, sometimes called the second love theory, rests on intimacy and compatibility but often lacks lasting commitment.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The third love theory says the final love adds a conscious decision to stay together through hard times.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This version circulates on blogs, and I could not trace it to a single peer-reviewed source. It does echo real research.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Fisher's three stages and Sternberg's three components overlap with it, and one popular writer presents Fisher's three loves as lust, passion and commitment.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Treat the "three loves in life" idea as a storytelling frame, not as science.&nbsp;</span></p>

<h2><strong>Are 3 types of love real? Sternberg's triangle</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The closest real science to "3 types of love" is Robert Sternberg's triangular theory of love, published in Psychological Review in 1986.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It describes three components: intimacy, passion and decision/commitment. Combining them gives different kinds of love.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For example, intimacy plus passion is romantic love, intimacy plus commitment is companionate love, and all three together is consummate love, which Sternberg treats as the fullest form.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Unlike the three stages of falling in love, which describe a sequence, the triangle describes a snapshot of what a relationship contains right now.</span></p>

<h2><strong>How do men fall in love? What the research says</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">How do men fall in love, and how fast?&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The best-known study is from psychologists Marissa Harrison and Jennifer Shortall, published in the Journal of Social Psychology in 2011.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Both men and women believed women fall in love first.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In reality, men reported falling in love sooner.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Of participants who had exchanged "I love you," a much greater share of men (64%) than women (18.51%) said they said it first, per a</span><a href="https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/homo-consumericus/201112/who-utters-i-love-you-first-men-or-women"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Psychology Today summary</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">So, how men fall in love psychology suggests, is often quickly and quietly.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A separate write-up argues the lag may be in words, not feelings, since men may recognize love early but take longer to say it.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Treat these results with care.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The study surveyed roughly 170 mostly college-aged, heterosexual participants and relied on memory of past relationships.</span></p>
<h2><strong>How long does it take a man to fall in love?</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">How long does it take a man to fall in love?&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In Harrison's study, according to MEL Magazine, men said they would know in about a few weeks, while women said a few months. Those are self-reported averages, not a rule.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Some men fall in days and some take over a year.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If you are asking how long does it take for a woman to fall in love, the same study suggests women often take a bit longer.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Harrison has theorized that women may postpone love until they are sure they have picked the right partner, though she and others note this is an interpretation and not a proven cause.</span></p>
<h2><strong>What makes a man fall in love?</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">What makes a man fall in love is not a single trigger.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Fisher notes that timing, state of health, access to resources, childhood experiences, and many cultural and biological forces all play a role in attraction.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">What makes men fall in love in a given relationship tends to include feeling admired, feeling safe, shared humor, and a sense that the person is both exciting and easy to be around.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Sternberg's triangular theory gives a useful lens here.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It says love has three components: intimacy (closeness), passion (attraction) and decision or commitment. A man is more likely to stay in love when all three are present, not only passion.</span></p>

<h2><strong>Is the honeymoon phase real?</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Is the honeymoon phase real?&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Yes, in the sense that early love feels more intense and idealized, and it fades.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Gottman Institute describes the honeymoon phase as serving an evolutionary purpose, bonding pairs long enough to reproduce and protect offspring, and says the</span><a href="https://www.gottman.com/blog/is-the-honeymoon-phase-a-myth-understanding-loves-most-romanticized-stage/"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">transition out of it is an opportunity</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to build something deeper.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">How long does it last?&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Experts quoted by Vogue Philippines say anywhere from a few weeks to a couple of years. Paired says research suggests 3 months to 2 years.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A 2015 study is often cited for about 30 months, but I only found that in secondary sources, so treat the figure as unverified.</span></p>
<h2><strong>What comes after the honeymoon phase in a relationship?</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">What comes after the honeymoon phase? Most relationship models say a power struggle, then stability.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">GoodTherapy lists five phases in romantic relationships: honeymoon, power struggle, stability, commitment and co-creation, with the power struggle showing up somewhere between two months and two years in.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">During that time, differences stand out and small issues feel bigger.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">What comes after the honeymoon phase in a relationship is not a failure.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A relationship expert quoted by Vogue says the phase after is where true emotional connection and intimacy are built.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This is where attachment, the third of Fisher's stages, takes over from attraction.</span></p>
<h2><strong>The 3 stages of relationships and other stages in a relationship</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If you want the 3 stages of relationships rather than the three stages of falling in love, a therapist's overview describes them as honeymoon (or limerence), power struggle, and mature secure attachment.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The stages of a relationship for a man follow the same arc as for anyone, and you will also see five-stage and seven-stage versions.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These stages in a relationship are descriptive tools.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">They don't arrive on a fixed schedule, and they are not always linear, per Paired.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Stages of a romantic relationship, stages of love and the 3 phases of love overlap, so use them as a rough map and not a timetable.</span></p>
